How to Compare Two Jeweller Quotes Fairly

One quote says 6% making, another says 10%. Which is cheaper? The honest answer is that you cannot tell until you put both on the same footing.

The short answer

Do not compare any single line, such as the making percentage. Convert both quotes to the same parts (rate, weight, making, wastage, stones and GST), work out each total, and compare the totals. The quote with the lowest headline number is often not the cheapest one.

Why quotes are hard to compare

Jewellers present the same piece in different ways. One shows a low making percentage but a higher gold rate. Another shows a flat making charge per gram. A third adds wastage. A fourth quotes a price that already includes GST. Each of these is a legitimate way to write a bill, but you cannot compare them line by line, because the pieces do not line up.

Step 1: Confirm you are quoting the same piece

Before comparing anything, make sure both quotes are for the same thing: the same weight, the same purity and the same type of finish. A piece that is 2 grams heavier or 18K instead of 22K will look different on paper for reasons that have nothing to do with the jeweller's pricing. Ask both jewellers for the net metal weight and the purity in writing.

Step 2: Put the rate on the same basis

Check that both quotes use the rate for the same purity and the same day. A rate that looks lower might be for 22K while the other is for 24K, which is not a fair comparison. If one jeweller says the rate will be fixed when you pay, ask what date that means.

Step 3: Convert making charges to a total

A percentage and a per-gram charge cannot be compared directly. Work out the rupee amount each one produces for your piece, and compare those. Remember that a percentage is applied to the metal value, so it depends on the rate that jeweller has used.

Step 4: Look for wastage and stone charges

Find out whether wastage is charged on top of making, and whether stones are priced separately. A quote that looks cheap may leave these out until the final bill, while a quote that looks expensive may already include them. Ask each jeweller whether the figure is the complete price.

Step 5: Add GST consistently

GST on gold jewellery is 3% of the total value including making charges, according to the GST Council's FAQ for the gems and jewellery sector. Make sure both quotes either include it or exclude it. Mixing a GST-inclusive quote with a GST-exclusive one makes the first look more expensive by exactly the GST amount.

A worked example

Here are two made-up quotes for a 10-gram 22K piece. Quote A advertises a lower making charge. Quote B advertises a lower gold rate. The numbers are for illustration only.

Quote A: “Only 6% making”

Gold value (10 g × ₹9,400)₹94,000
Wastage (4%)₹3,760
Making (6%)₹5,640
Subtotal₹1,03,400
GST (3%)₹3,102
Total₹1,06,502

Quote B: “Lower rate, 10% making”

Gold value (10 g × ₹9,200)₹92,000
Wastage₹0
Making (10%)₹9,200
Subtotal₹1,01,200
GST (3%)₹3,036
Total₹1,04,236

Quote A looked cheaper because of its lower making percentage, but its higher gold rate and its wastage line push the total up by about ₹2,266. The headline number told you nothing. The total told you everything.

What else matters besides price

The cheapest total is not always the best choice. Think also about the quality of finish, the hallmark, how the jeweller treats exchange or return, and whether you will get a proper bill. If two totals are close, the jeweller you trust and who explains the bill clearly may be worth a small difference.

When a customer comes back with a competitor's quote, the strongest answer is a clear breakdown of your own. A bill that separates the rate, making, wastage, stones and GST makes it easy for the customer to see exactly what they are paying for. Frequently asked questions

Is a lower making charge always cheaper?

No. A lower making percentage can be offset by a higher gold rate or by a wastage charge. Compare the final totals, not one line.

Should I compare quotes including or excluding GST?

Either, as long as both are treated the same way. Mixing one with GST and one without makes the comparison unfair.

How do I compare a percentage making charge with a per-gram charge?

Work out the rupee amount each produces for your piece, using that jeweller's own rate, and compare the rupee amounts.

Do I need the same piece from both jewellers?

Yes. Compare the same weight, purity and finish. Otherwise the difference in price may come from the piece and not from the jeweller's pricing.

What if the totals are very close?

Then other things matter more: hallmark, finish, how clear the bill is, exchange and return policy, and how comfortable you feel with the jeweller.

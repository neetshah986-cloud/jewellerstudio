How the value is worked out

  1. Net weight = total weight − stones and other weight.
  2. Metal value = net weight × rate per gram (adjusted for purity).
  3. Deduction = a percentage of the metal value.
  4. Exchange value = metal value − deduction − other charges.
  5. Balance = price of new jewellery − exchange value.

Example: 100 g of sterling (925) silver using a fine silver rate of ₹100. The rate adjusts to ₹92.59, so the metal value is ₹9,259.26. With a 4% deduction the exchange value is ₹8,888.89.

Common questions

How is the exchange value worked out?

Net weight = total weight minus the weight of stones or other non-metal parts. Metal value = net weight × the rate per gram for your purity. The jeweller’s deduction and any other charges are then subtracted to give the exchange value.

Why is the exchange value lower than the market rate?

Jewellers usually deduct for melting or refining loss, handling and old-jewellery charges. The deduction differs from one jeweller to another, so ask what it is and get it in writing before you decide.

Should I weigh the stones separately?

Yes. Stones, pearls and other non-metal parts are not valued as silver. Subtract their weight, as the jeweller will. Ask whether any stones are valued separately.

Is exchanging better than selling for cash?

It depends on the jeweller’s terms. Some give a better value when you exchange against a new purchase, others do not. Enter both deductions in the calculator to compare.

How do I know the purity of my old silver?

Look for a stamp such as 925 (sterling) or 999 (fine silver). Many old silver items have no stamp, so ask the jeweller to test the purity in front of you and note it on the slip. Then set “Your old silver purity” to match.

Does this include taxes?

No. Taxes on the new purchase and any charges on the bill are not included. Please confirm the final figures with the jeweller or your accountant.

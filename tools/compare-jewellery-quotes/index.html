How the comparison works

  1. Each quote = metal value + wastage + making + other charges + GST.
  2. Pure metal = weight × purity (for example 916 ÷ 1000 for 22K gold).
  3. Cost per pure gram = total ÷ pure metal, so different weights and purities can be compared fairly.
  4. Extras share = making + wastage + other, as a percentage of the metal value.

Example: two quotes for 10 g of 22K gold. A: rate ₹4,600 with 8% making. B: rate ₹4,550 with 12% making. With 3% GST, A comes to ₹51,170.40 and B to ₹52,488.80, so A is cheaper by ₹1,318.40, even though B quoted the lower rate.

Common questions

Why compare cost per gram of pure metal?

Two pieces can have different weights or purity, so the totals are not comparable. Pure metal content = weight × purity. Dividing the total by pure grams shows what you pay for each gram of actual gold or silver, including making, wastage, other charges and GST.

A lower rate per gram does not always mean cheaper. Why?

A jeweller can quote a lower metal rate and recover it through higher making charges or wastage. This tool adds everything together, so you can see the all-in price. The share of extras on the metal value shows how much is charged beyond the metal itself.

What should I compare?

Compare pieces of the same purity, with the rate, making charges, wastage, stone charges and GST written on each quote. Ask for these in writing, and check the hallmark and the weight on the final bill.

Does this tell me which jeweller is better?

No. It only compares the numbers you enter. Trust, design, service, buyback terms and certification also matter when you choose a jeweller.

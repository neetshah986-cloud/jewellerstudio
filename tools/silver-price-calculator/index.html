How silver price is worked out

  1. Silver value = weight × rate per gram (adjusted for purity).
  2. Making charges = % of the value, or ₹ per gram.
  3. GST = rate × (silver + making + other).
  4. Total = subtotal + GST.

Example: 100 g of fine silver at ₹100 per gram with 10% making and 3% GST. Silver ₹10,000 + making ₹1,000 = ₹11,000. GST ₹330. Total ₹11,330.

Common questions

What is 925 sterling silver?

Sterling silver is 92.5% pure silver (925) mixed with other metals for strength. Fine silver is 99.9% (999). Most silver jewellery is 925, while 800 silver is also used in some articles.

How does the calculator adjust for purity?

If you enter the fine silver (999) rate but choose 925 jewellery, it scales the rate by the purity ratio (925 ÷ 999). If your jeweller quotes the 925 rate directly, set both dropdowns to 925.

Why are silver making charges often higher?

Silver is cheaper per gram than gold, so the crafting cost makes up a bigger share of the price. That is why making charges are often a higher percentage, or a fixed ₹ per gram. Use whichever your jeweller quotes.

What GST rate should I use?

Jewellery is commonly billed with 3% GST on the value of the metal plus making charges. The rate is editable in the calculator, so you can change it. Please confirm the rate for your case with your jeweller or accountant.

Where do I get today’s rate?

Use the rate shown by your jeweller or local bullion association for the same day. This calculator does not fetch live rates, so you stay in control of the number that goes into the calculation.

How accurate is the result?

It is an estimate based on the numbers you enter. The final bill can differ with the jeweller’s own rates, rounding, hallmarking charges and any other charges.
